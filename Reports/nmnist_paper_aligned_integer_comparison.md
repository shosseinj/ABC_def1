## Large-gap diagnostic: B∞=1 and B0=200

The audited local `B∞=1` attack moves 585–2,029 cell-packets per example (mean 1,599.142) by exactly one bin, and succeeds on 1,000/1,000. A maximum shift of one bin does not limit how many packets move. The audited local `B0=200` attack moves exactly 200 packets per example, with maximum shifts of 8–9 bins and mean total displacement `B1=851.562`; it succeeds on 945/1,000. Thus these two budget families do not represent equal perturbation sizes. The audits establish that the attacks obey the local packet-level constraints, but they do not identify why our model differs from the published architectures or prove matched training. At upstream commit `f16738f80be94067223f4968c1c318a125b9a321`, `utils/attack.py` hard-codes `5 * over_b.mean() / max(1, l0_moves_budget)` in `PGDTimeShiftAfterEncoder_L0`; Section 5 of the paper states general `λbudget=10` unless noted. The local adapter uses the upstream class, so changing the JSON alone would not change this coefficient. The paper/code difference must be resolved before claiming an exact B0 recipe match.

**Comparison status: `NON_COMPARABLE` for a fair four-model performance claim.** The shared dataset, grid, and attack budgets allow an exploratory juxtaposition only. Our training uses a local 55,000/5,000 stratified train/validation split, Adam at learning rate 0.001, weight decay 0.0001, batch size 64, up to 15 epochs, and validation-based checkpoint selection. The paper identifies directly trained SNNs but does not provide a complete N-MNIST training recipe or the N-MNIST model checkpoints in the available source. Therefore matching the three reference models' training, preprocessing details, and attacked sample identities cannot be established from these artifacts. A further paper/code attack ambiguity remains for B0: upstream code fixes its penalty at 5, while the paper states general `λbudget=10`. Our architecture, checkpoint, and clean accuracy also differ, and only one local seed was run. These numbers do not establish a controlled architecture ranking or a multi-seed robustness claim.
